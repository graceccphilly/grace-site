Your Philadelphia wedding officiant

Rev. Arian B. Harris

For over 20 years, Rev. Harris has officiated weddings across Philadelphia and its suburbs, standing beside couples on one of the biggest days of their lives. He is the pastor of Grace Community Church in Philadelphia and holds a Master of Divinity in Pastoral Counseling.

His ceremonies are warm, unhurried and personal. He takes time to learn your story, helps you choose words that sound like you, and keeps the day calm when nerves run high. He also takes care of the legal side: he signs your Pennsylvania license and returns it on time, so your marriage is recorded.

How to book your wedding officiant

From first message to signed license, in six steps.

  1. Request your date

    Fill in the request form below. It takes about five minutes and is not a commitment.

  2. Free 20-minute call

    Rev. Harris confirms he is free, answers your questions and recommends a package.

  3. Hold your date

    Sign a short agreement and pay a deposit. Your date is then reserved for you.

  4. Plan your ceremony

    Fill in the ceremony planning questionnaire, then meet to shape the words, readings and vows.

  5. Get your license

    Apply together in person at least a week before the wedding. See the license facts below.

  6. Say “I do”

    Rev. Harris leads the ceremony, signs your license with you and returns it to the county within 10 days.

How to get a Philadelphia marriage license

You need a license before the ceremony. Here is what the City asks for.

The essentials

Regular license
$90
Paid by Visa, Mastercard or money order. No cash or personal checks.
Waiting period
3 days
The license is valid on the third day after you apply.
License is good for
60 days
Anywhere in Pennsylvania, but not in another state.

Where to apply

Both of you go in person to the Marriage License Department, City Hall, Room 413, 1400 John F. Kennedy Boulevard, Philadelphia. Each of you brings two forms of identification, one with a photo.

When to apply

Apply at least a week before your wedding, and no more than 60 days before it. If the 60 days run out, you have to reapply and pay again.

After the ceremony

Rev. Harris signs the license and returns it to the Marriage License Department within 10 days, as the law requires. You do not have to do anything else.

Source: City of Philadelphia, Get a marriage license (opens in a new tab). Fees and rules can change, so check the City’s page before you go.

Wedding officiant FAQs

Do we have to be members of Grace Community Church?

No. Rev. Harris marries couples from every background, whether or not you attend Grace or any church.

Can our ceremony be interfaith or non-religious?

Yes. Rev. Harris leads Christian ceremonies, ceremonies that honor two faith traditions, and ceremonies with no religious content at all. You decide what is said.

How far ahead should we book?

Saturdays from May to October fill first, so three to nine months ahead is best. Elopements and weekday ceremonies can often be booked a few weeks out. Send a request even if your date is close.

Where do you perform ceremonies?

At your venue: a church, hall, park, home or restaurant in Philadelphia, Bucks, Chester, Delaware or Montgomery County. Farther venues are quoted.

Do we tip the officiant?

No tip is expected; the package price covers his time. If you would like to give something extra, a gift to Grace Community Church supports our ministry.

What if we need to change the date?

Tell us as soon as you can. If Rev. Harris is free on the new date, your deposit moves with you.
